Mzizi's doctrine is data in [`mzizi-dev/mzizi-registry`](https://github.com/mzizi-dev/mzizi-registry),
under `content/doctrine/`. It has three parts, each listed below in the order the registry
sorts it. The names, Shona words and meanings are the registry's own wording. `get_doctrine` serves the pillars and principles; the engineering constraints are not yet served over MCP or the API.

These are not the four design pillars in [the foundations overview](/foundations/overview).
Those are the design system's own decisions. The Ubuntu pillars below are the doctrine they
rest on.

## The five Ubuntu pillars

Doctrine version `4.1.0`. Source: Mugumbate et al. (2023). The first four are the horizontal
pillars; spirituality is the vertical one.

| #   | Pillar       | Shona            | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| --- | ------------ | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Family       | Mhuri            | The first sphere of Ubuntu — the household and kin who form a person's primary web of belonging. Ubuntu begins here: identity is given by family before it is given by any institution.                                                |
| 2   | Community    | Nharaunda        | The extended sphere of neighbours, peers, and chosen kin. Where the family ends, the community begins; the two are continuous, not opposed. Ubuntu treats community as the medium through which a person grows into society.           |
| 3   | Society      | Vanhu            | The broader human web — strangers, institutions, the polity. Ubuntu does not stop at the village wall; vanhu means people, all people. A person who treats strangers as strangers has not yet practised Ubuntu.                        |
| 4   | Environment  | Zvakatipoteredza | The natural world that holds all relationships. Ubuntu is ecological before it is social — soil, water, and weather are kin too. A person who exhausts the environment has broken Ubuntu, even if they have wronged no human directly. |
| 5   | Spirituality | Unhu             | The vertical axis of being human — meaning, purpose, ancestry, and the sacred. Unhu names the inner quality from which Ubuntu flows outward. Without unhu, the four horizontal pillars are scaffolding without a soul.                 |

## The five Ubuntu principles

Doctrine version `4.1.0`.

| #   | Principle  | Shona       | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Source                                                                        |
| --- | ---------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Survival   | Kurarama    | Collective endurance. No one survives alone; basic needs are met through one another. Reciprocal care sustains the community before any individual can flourish.                                                     | Mbigi (1997), Collective Fingers Theory                                       |
| 2   | Solidarity | Kubatana    | A firm and persevering commitment to the common good. Not vague compassion but active allegiance to the welfare of the whole — kubatana means to hold together.                                                      | Mbigi (1997), Collective Fingers Theory                                       |
| 3   | Compassion | Tsitsi      | Active care, sympathy, and concern shown through helping, sharing, and welcoming. Tsitsi extends beyond kin to all — a stranger in need is not a stranger to compassion. (Also: Du Toit, Poovan & Engelbretch 2006.) | Mbigi (1997), Collective Fingers Theory; Du Toit, Poovan & Engelbretch (2006) |
| 4   | Respect    | Ruremekedzo | Unprejudiced consideration of another's privileges, beliefs, and norms. Encompasses elder-respect (ukuhlonipha) but applies to every person, every age, every belief. (Also: Poovan et al. 2006.)                    | Mbigi (1997), Collective Fingers Theory; Poovan et al. (2006)                 |
| 5   | Dignity    | Chiremerera | Inherent worth held by virtue of existence — irrespective of status, ethnicity, age, or background. Humanity is sacred. Dignity is not earned and cannot be revoked. (Also: Ramose 2002.)                            | Mbigi (1997), Collective Fingers Theory; Ramose (2002)                        |

## The seven engineering constraints

Doctrine version `4.2.0`. The owner added these on 17 November 2026
([mzizi-registry#723](https://github.com/mzizi-dev/mzizi-registry/pull/723)). Each one has a
check, which is how it is held to account.

| #   | Constraint                     | What it means                                                                                                                                                                            | How it is checked                                                                                                                                                |
| --- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Outdoor readability            | Every surface is read in direct sunlight on a budget phone, not only at a desk.                                                                                                          | Contrast is checked against the Mzizi floor in light and dark themes (`mzizi_check_accessibility`); a pair under the floor fails.                                |
| 2   | Connectivity-gradient design   | Design for the whole range of connections, from none to fibre. Offline-first is the enforced minimum: core tasks work with no connection, and better connectivity adds to that baseline. | A surface whose core tasks do not work offline fails. Core tasks are also tested on a throttled 2G profile.                                                      |
| 3   | Multilingual parity            | Shona, Ndebele and English are equal. No language is a translation of another, and none ships later.                                                                                     | A missing translation fails the build.                                                                                                                           |
| 4   | Shared-device awareness        | One phone often serves a household. A person's data belongs to that person, not to the device.                                                                                           | Sign-out leaves no personal data on the device; a second account on the same device cannot read the first's.                                                     |
| 5   | Personal data sovereignty      | A person owns their data. It lives on their device first, syncs to their own isolated cloud space, and moves to pods in the Nyuchi Honeycomb without being reshaped.                     | Personal data is stored on the device and in the person's isolated space only; a person can export and delete all of it.                                         |
| 6   | No surveillance business model | We do not earn money from attention or personal data.                                                                                                                                    | No third-party tracking, data sale or engagement-maximising pattern is present; review fails a change that adds one.                                             |
| 7   | The Bundu floor                | The minimums nothing can go under: text size, contrast, hit regions and spacing, so that nobody is punished for their eyesight or their device.                                          | The existing `bundu-floor` doctrine (`get_doctrine` with `topic: bundu-floor`) and `mzizi_check_accessibility`. See [the Bundu floor](/foundations/bundu-floor). |

Check names are the registry's. Over MCP, call `mzizi_execute` with `operation: "check_accessibility"`.

Each constraint also names the principles it rests on in the registry. Survival and
connectivity, respect and multilingual parity, and dignity and personal data sovereignty are
examples of that link. The registry holds the full mapping.
